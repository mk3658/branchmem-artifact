# Protocol: Human Adjudication of the Discrimination Bank

**Status: RUN.** Three annotators, none an author of the bank, completed all
100 items. The filled sheets are in `discrimination_sheets/`, the scored output
in `results/final/discrimination_human_adjudication.json`, and the analysis is
reproduced by:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/score_discrimination_adjudication.py
```

Headline outcomes, against the plan fixed in advance below:

- Agreement: raw all-three 0.82, Fleiss kappa 0.741, nominal Krippendorff alpha
  0.741.
- **Q3, the decisive test: the convention is upheld.** 0 of 25 constraint items
  were judged the user's alone to lift; 19 required someone else's agreement, 6
  were physical limits.
- **Q2 validates the type assignment**: 25/25 constraint ancestors judged
  standing rules, 24/25 override ancestors ordinary values, 25/25
  independent-observation ancestors ordinary values.
- **The central claim holds on a measure defined the same way for people and
  policies.** Take-the-update gap between the two critical types: +1.000,
  +0.640, +1.000 for the three annotators against +.000 to +.280 for every
  policy tested. By majority, annotators took the update on 0/25 constraints
  and 25/25 overrides.
- **Two construct problems were found in the bank, both against us.** The
  constraint target "keep the stored value" is stronger than people are, who
  preferred flagging (7 kept, 9 flagged, 9 split three ways, 0 took the
  update). And the `ambiguous` type is **not** ambiguous to people: 0 of 25
  drew a majority "flag for review", and 21/25 of those ancestors were judged
  ordinary values. That category does not measure calibrated abstention and the
  paper now says so.

A successor bank should drop the `ambiguous` type and score constraints as "do
not take the update" rather than "keep". This bank is frozen and preregistered,
so neither change was made to it.

## Why this study exists

The discrimination experiment's claim depends on one labelling convention: when
the stored entry records a standing rule and an update would violate it, the
correct merge keeps the rule, even though the update is user-sourced and carries
confidence 0.95. One objection is that this is a normative choice rather than a
fact, because users often have authority over their own constraints. If that
is right, a merger that takes the update is applying a defensible competing norm
and the paper's finding is a disagreement about labels, not a result about
models.

Two things are already known without human labels, both reported in the paper:

- `scripts/analyze_label_norms.py` classifies all 80 constraint ancestors by who
  could lift the constraint. 70 name an external party (a lease, a building
  rule, a service contract, an insurer, a venue, an employer, a union, a
  library), 9 state a physical or capacity ceiling, and 1 is unclassified. None
  is a commitment the user could withdraw unilaterally. The classification, the
  phrase that decided each case, and the full table are in
  `discrimination_authority_locus.csv`.
- Granting the objection outright and re-scoring with "take the user-sourced
  update" as correct for both critical types moves balanced accuracy to 0.956
  for `gpt-5.4` and 1.000 for the confidence rule. So the sign of the result
  does depend on the convention, which is exactly why it should be checked by
  people rather than argued.

This study checks it. Question 3 is the decisive one.

## Population and sample

100 items drawn from the frozen bank
(`data/discrimination_bank.json`, sha256 pinned in
`data/discrimination_bank.sha256`), 25 of each of the four conflict types, each
type's 25 items taken from 25 distinct template families: 61 distinct families in
all. Sample seed 9091, fixed before the draw. Generated by
`scripts/make_discrimination_adjudication_sheets.py`; re-running it reproduces
the identical sample.

## Annotators

Three independent adult annotators proficient in English, none an author of the
bank and none shown this document's "Why this study exists" section before
annotating. Record a random annotator ID only; collect no demographic
information. Pay at or above the local minimum wage for the time taken.

## Blinding

- Our label is never shown. The key is in
  `discrimination_sheets/discrimination_key_HELD_BACK.csv` and must not be given
  to annotators.
- Which physical branch carries the stronger provenance is randomised per item,
  so "A is always the user-sourced one" is false.
- The four conflict types are interleaved in a shuffled order, so the type
  cannot be inferred from position.
- Annotators see what a merger sees: the ancestor value, both branch values, and
  each branch's source and confidence. The provenance assignment is the primary
  arm's: the update side always carries `user`/0.95 and the other side
  `inference`/0.55, so the metadata cannot separate the two critical types.

## Questions

Per item, in `discrimination_sheets/discrimination_annotator{1,2,3}.csv`:

1. `q1_which_value_should_be_stored` — `a`, `b`, or `neither_flag_for_review`.
   The merge decision. "Neither" is a first-class answer, not a refusal: some
   items are built so that no determinate answer exists.
2. `q2_ancestor_is` — `standing_rule`, `ordinary_value`, or `unclear`. Is the
   ancestor entry a rule that remains in force until explicitly withdrawn, or an
   ordinary value that a later, better update may replace? This is the
   classification the paper's two-stage mitigation performs in its first stage.
3. `q3_who_could_lift_it` — `user_alone`, `someone_else`, `nobody_physical`, or
   `unclear`; answer only when Q2 is `standing_rule`. **This is that
   objection, stated as a measurable question.**
4. `q4_confidence_1to5` — 1 is a guess, 5 is certain.
5. `notes` — free text, optional.

Annotators should be told only that they are deciding which value an assistant's
memory should keep after two offline copies disagreed, and that timestamps are
unavailable because the copies had no shared clock.

## Analysis, fixed in advance

Compute and report all of the following, whichever way they come out:

1. **Q1 against the construction labels**, per type: agreement rate, and
   Krippendorff's alpha across the three annotators. The construction labels are
   supported only if agreement on `immutable_constraint` is high.
2. **Q3 is the decisive test.** Report the distribution of `user_alone` versus
   `someone_else`/`nobody_physical` over the 25 constraint items. If a majority
   of annotators say `user_alone` for a majority of those items, the paper's
   convention is wrong, and the discrimination result must be restated as
   conditional on a norm that people do not share. Say so in those words.
3. **Re-score every model** against the annotator majority on Q1 in place of the
   construction label, as `scripts/analyze_human_label_robustness.py` already
   does for the earlier banks, and report whether any comparison changes sign.
4. **Q2 against our type assignment**, as an independent check that the two
   critical types differ in the way the bank intends.
5. Report per-item disagreements rather than only aggregates, and release the
   filled sheets.

Items where annotators split three ways are reported as such and not resolved by
an author.

## What would falsify the paper's claim

If annotators judge, by majority, that the user alone could lift most of the 25
constraints, or if re-scoring against their Q1 majority removes the
discrimination gap, then the result is an artifact of our labelling convention.
We commit to reporting either outcome in the paper rather than relegating it to
an appendix.
